## Question, not conclusion

If Health Canada determines that the frozen product is SaMD, the manufacturer
asks which class and then-current Schedule 1 rule(s) apply to its exact intended
use and enforced exclusions. Counsel must analyze the current SaMD
significance/situation framework and the current text and interpretation of
Rules 10(1), 10(2), 12 and any other applicable provision. No statutory pin-cite
is approved in this source.

## Decision factors for Health Canada/counsel

- The result informs an immediate medication-preparation task but does not
  select, recommend or verify treatment.
- Wrong arithmetic or reliance could contribute to serious injury or death.
- The approved user, setting, patient, medication and workflow scope may affect
  whether the situation is non-serious, serious or critical.
- The transparency/review mechanism may affect reliance but does not erase the
  immediate-use context or substitute for usability evidence.
- Exclusions are relevant only if they are approved, clear, enforceable and
  consistent with foreseeable use and all claims.

## Conservative fallback position

Until Health Canada or qualified regulatory counsel supports a lower route,
planning treats Class III as possible where broad or critical use could make
the output drive management in a critical situation. The final analysis must
also candidly test Class I and Class II rationales against the same facts; the
manufacturer must not narrow the wording merely to obtain a preferred class.

Record Health Canada's exact wording, assumptions and cited rule(s). A
classification-support response is not an MDL, MDEL or authorization to market.
Controlled sources: REG-003 and RMF-001, with REG-001/REG-002 supplying the
approved scope and VVR-001 supplying exact-build evidence when available.
